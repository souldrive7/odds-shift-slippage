### Correction found at session close (2026-09-12 evening)

This machine is the **Alienware m18 R2 (AW)**, not the mothership. The arms listed as "missing" above
are exactly the ones trained on the ASUS ExpertBook (**EB**, the mothership in `msc-thesis/docs/aw_setup.md`)
and never copied here: every array that exists here is one AW trained or received via Drive, and every
one of those matches its canonical hash. So the canonical arrays are most likely intact on EB, and the
"retrain check" only shows that AW's LightGBM run differs from EB's. `uq_arrays.npz` (the three thesis
scorers) was restored from the DVC remote and matches its canonical hash exactly.

Recovery path (no recomputation needed): on EB run `scripts/transfer_arrays.ps1` (writes the 11 missing
LightGBM arms and the MLP arms to the Drive transfer folder with TRANSFER.json manifests); on AW run
`python scripts/receive_arrays.py` (verifies every SHA-256 against `_meta.inputs` and places the files).
Once every array MATCHes, option 2 below becomes "archive the complete canonical set on Zenodo" and the
paper's digits are verifiable without changing any number. The Limitations sentence about the retrained
pair should then be reworded: it documents run-to-run variation across two machines, not a lost artefact.

### Consequence for reproducibility (as understood before the correction above)

The headline Santander and Instacart numbers come from single runs whose arrays no longer exist, and a
third party who retrains from the public Kaggle data will obtain arrays like the retrained ones: the same
qualitative picture (the draws in Supplement S7 already show the weighted raw score moving by ±0.05),
but not the printed digits. The result JSON, its `_meta.inputs` hashes and the paper's macros are
internally consistent; what is missing is any file that matches those hashes.

Options, in order of strength (decision pending):

1. **Regenerate the whole LightGBM evidence from arrays that exist, and archive them.** Retrain the
   missing Instacart arms (7 configs; the Santander four are already retrained), rerun every
   `run_experiment.py` part for both datasets (`ladder`, `whatif`, `bootstrap`, `deploy`, `calsize`,
   `dead_freq`, `tie`, `capsweep`, `seeds`), regenerate macros, re-read every sentence whose number
   moved, deposit the complete array set (~30 GB uncompressed, less as float16/npz) on Zenodo, and point
   `DATA.md` at the DOI. Every number in the paper is then regenerable from files anyone can download.
   Cost: roughly a day of compute on this machine plus a text pass; the three thesis scorers stay
   unreproducible and would be dropped from Supplement Table S1/S2/S4.
2. **Archive what exists and say so.** Deposit the surviving arrays (including the retrained four and the
   twins) on Zenodo, keep the canonical numbers, and state in `DATA.md` and the Limitations that the
   canonical uncapped pair arrays are lost and the retrained pair is provided instead. Cheap, honest,
   but the headline digits remain unverifiable.
3. **Leave as is** (hash-pinned, not shipped). Weakest; a reviewer who asks for the arrays cannot be
   given files that match the paper.
